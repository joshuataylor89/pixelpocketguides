What makes a handheld Game Boy-style?

These devices copy the classic shapes: a tall pocket-sized body with a D-pad and buttons below the screen (like the original Game Boy), or a fold-up flip design (like the Game Boy Advance SP). They are cheap, easy to carry, and built for 8-bit, 16-bit and handheld classics. They do not have analog sticks, so for N64, PSP or PS2 games look at our dual-joystick handhelds.

How to choose

  • Original Game Boy shape: the TRIMUI Brick and the RG35XX Plus.
  • Game Boy Advance SP shape: the RG35XXSP. For a faster flip, see Miyoo Flip vs RG35XXSP.
  • Real cartridges: the Analogue Pocket plays original Game Boy, Game Boy Color and Game Boy Advance cartridges.
  • Games and storage: most emulation handhelds ship without games, so you supply backups of games you own. Add a microSD card from our microSD card guide.

Quick answers

What is the best Game Boy-style handheld?

For most people, the TRIMUI Brick. It has a sharp screen, a simple operating system and a pocket-friendly size.

What is the best Game Boy-style handheld for real cartridges?

The Analogue Pocket. It plays original Game Boy, Game Boy Color and Game Boy Advance cartridges.

What is the cheapest Game Boy-style handheld?

The Anbernic RG35XX Plus is one of the cheapest solid options, often around $50 to $70.

Is there a Game Boy Advance SP-style handheld?

Yes, the Anbernic RG35XXSP folds shut like the GBA SP.
