What is a horizontal retro handheld?

A horizontal (or landscape) handheld is the wide, controller-style shape. Because the buttons sit beside the screen, there is space for analog sticks, triggers and a larger battery, which is why most Android retro handhelds use this layout. They run emulators for systems like the Sega Dreamcast, Sony PSP, Nintendo GameCube and PlayStation 2, and many can also play Android games and stream games from the cloud.

Horizontal vs vertical handhelds: which should you buy?

  • Choose horizontal if you want analog sticks, a bigger screen, and the power for Dreamcast, PSP, GameCube or PS2 games.
  • Choose vertical if you want a cheap, pocket-sized Game Boy-style handheld for 8-bit, 16-bit and PS1 games. See our Top 5 Vertical Retro Handhelds.
  • Want all the options? Our Top 10 Retro Handhelds for the Holidays and the buying guide by price cover every shape and budget.

How to choose a horizontal retro handheld

  • Power. Stronger chips run PS2 and GameCube games at higher resolutions. If you mostly play older systems, a cheaper model is enough.
  • Screen shape and size. A 4:3 screen suits classic games, a square screen suits Game Boy and DS games, and a large 16:9 screen suits PSP and modern games.
  • Operating system. These run Android, which needs more setup than a Linux handheld but runs more emulators and apps.
  • Games. Most emulation handhelds ship without games. You supply backups of games you own. The Analogue Pocket plays your real cartridges.
  • Storage and budget. Prices here run from about $110 to over $400, so check Amazon for the current price. Many models take a microSD card. Browse microSD cards on Amazon or read our microSD card guide.
