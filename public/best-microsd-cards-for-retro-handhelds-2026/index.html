microSD Cards for Retro Handhelds Guide

Updated November 10, 2026

The first thing you will want to do with a retro handheld is remove the cheap, unbranded microSD card it ships with and upgrade. Almost every retro handheld needs a microSD card for games, saves and emulator files, and the card you pick affects how fast games load and how safe your saves are. Here are 10 microSD card brands that work well in retro handhelds, from the trusted favorites to budget picks, all sold on Amazon.

Short on time? For most handhelds, buy a 256GB Samsung or SanDisk card. They are the safest, easiest choices.

How much storage do you need?

  • 128GB: plenty for 8-bit, 16-bit, Game Boy Advance and most PS1 libraries.
  • 256GB: the sweet spot for most people. Covers big PS1 collections, PSP and DS plus extras.
  • 512GB: for PS2, GameCube and Dreamcast libraries, which take much more space. Some handhelds list a maximum card size, so check yours before buying.
  • 1TB: room for a huge library of everything, with space left over. Confirm your handheld supports a card this large first, since many cap card size.

Speed labels in plain English

  • U1 / U3: minimum write speed. U3 is a safe choice for anything.
  • A1 / A2: better at running apps. Useful for Android handhelds that load games from the card.
  • Class 10: fine for Linux handhelds that mostly run small retro games.

Cards advertise their top speed, but a handheld may not use all of it. A mid-priced card is usually enough.

How to avoid fake microSD cards

  • Buy cards that are sold and shipped by Amazon, or by the brand's official store.
  • Be suspicious of an unusually low price on a large card, especially 512GB and above.
  • Check the card as soon as it arrives with a free tool such as H2testw (Windows) or F3 (Mac and Linux), and return it quickly if it fails.
  • Keep a backup of your saves. Any card can fail.

Setting up your card

  • Most handhelds work with FAT32 or exFAT. If your handheld asks for one, follow its manual.
  • Large cards sometimes arrive in exFAT. Check what your handheld supports before copying games over.
  • Handhelds with two slots let you use one card for the system and one for games.
