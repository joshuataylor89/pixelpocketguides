Before you buy: gift-giving tips

  • Bring your own games. Most emulation handhelds ship without games. Players use backups of games they own. The Evercade Super Pocket and the Analogue Pocket are the exceptions, since they use licensed built-in games or real cartridges.
  • Add a microSD card. Most of these use one for storage, and it is the one accessory that makes the gift usable on day one. Browse microSD cards on Amazon.
  • Check who sells it. Buy from the brand's own storefront or a seller with solid reviews. Rebranded clones with big markups are common in this category.
  • Linux or Android? Linux handhelds (TRIMUI Brick, RG35XXSP, RG28XX) are simple and fast to set up. Android handhelds (RG Cube, Odin 2, RG477M) need more setup but run PS2, GameCube and mobile games.

Quick answers

What is the best retro handheld for a beginner?

The TRIMUI Brick or the RG35XXSP. Both run simple Linux software and play Game Boy through PS1 without fuss. For zero setup at all, the Evercade Super Pocket comes with its games built in.

Which handheld plays PS2 games?

The AYN Odin 2 and Anbernic RG477M are the strongest picks here. The RG Cube can run many PS2 games with some stutter.

The TRIMUI Brick for pocketability, the RG35XXSP for a clamshell, and the MagicX Zero 40 for DS games. Our buying guide by price goes deeper.
