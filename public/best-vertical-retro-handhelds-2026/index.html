What is a vertical retro handheld?

A vertical handheld (also called a portrait or Game Boy-style handheld) holds the screen above the buttons instead of between them. That keeps the device narrow and pocketable, and it is the shape most people picture when they think of a retro gaming handheld. Most vertical emulation handhelds run Linux with a simple menu, so you can pick it up and play with little setup, while a few, like the MagicX Zero 40, run Android.

Vertical vs horizontal handhelds: which should you buy?

  • Choose vertical if you want a pocket-sized, lightweight handheld for 8-bit, 16-bit, Game Boy Advance and PlayStation 1 games, or if you are shopping for a kid or a nostalgic gift.
  • Choose horizontal if you want analog sticks, a bigger screen, or PS2, GameCube and PSP performance. See our Top 10 Retro Handhelds for the Holidays.
  • Choose a clamshell if you want a fold-up screen protector. The buying guide by price covers the best option.

How to choose a vertical handheld

  • Screen size and shape. Smaller screens (around 2.8 to 3.5 inches) suit Game Boy and SNES games and fit any pocket. Larger ones suit DS games and arcade shooters.
  • Operating system. Linux handhelds are simple to start with, and Android handhelds can run more apps.
  • Games. Most emulation handhelds ship without games. You supply backups of games you own. The Evercade Super Pocket comes with licensed games built in.
  • Battery and storage. Check the battery size, and buy a microSD card for your games. Browse microSD cards on Amazon or read our microSD card guide.
  • Budget. Prices here run from about $40 to under $90. Always check Amazon for the current price, since sales around Black Friday and Cyber Monday can change it.
