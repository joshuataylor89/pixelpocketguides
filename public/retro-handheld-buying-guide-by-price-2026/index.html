Under $50: stocking-stuffer territory

At this price you are buying a small, simple handheld for 8-bit, 16-bit and PlayStation 1 games. Expect compromises, and keep expectations modest.

Best pick: Anbernic RG28XX · Often under $40

A tiny, cheap handheld that fits in any pocket. Truly pocketable, Game Boy Micro style; small and a bit cramped.

Check price on Amazon →

Skip it if you have big hands or want PS2. The shoulder buttons rattle and the layout is cramped. See the full write-up.

$50–$100: the sweet spot

Retro Catalog notes that this bracket gives the most for your money. Light, cheap hardware is more than enough for classic consoles, and it opens up Dreamcast, N64 and PSP on some models.

Best pick: TRIMUI Brick · Around $70

A sleek, pocket-sized handheld that just works. Sharp, bright screen for its size; no analog sticks (D-pad can mimic one).

$100–$200: where Android gets interesting

Here you start to see Android handhelds with real sticks, bigger screens and enough power for some PS2 and GameCube games. Setup takes longer than on Linux devices, but the flexibility is worth it for the right person.

Best pick: Anbernic RG Cube · Around $170

One device that covers everything from Game Boy to PS2. Square screen suits Game Boy, arcade and DS games without stretching; square screen is not ideal for widescreen games.

Best value: AYANEO Pocket Air Mini · From about $110

Android flexibility and premium build on a budget. Build quality that feels like a more expensive device; thick around the grips.

Before you check out

  • Games are not included. Emulation handhelds need game files, which you should only make from games you own. The Super Pocket (built-in licensed games) and the Analogue Pocket (real cartridges) avoid this.
  • Get a microSD card. It is what the games live on. Browse microSD cards on Amazon or read our microSD card guide.
  • Check the seller and return window. Buy from the brand or a well-reviewed seller, and keep the return window in mind when giving a gift.
  • Prices move. The figures here are reviewer-reported, so check Amazon for the live price.
